DIFFICULTY AND FIDELITY — ICDC TIER
This block SUPERSEDES the "DIFFICULTY (scale to the provided competition level)" section
above. Ignore that section's District / State / ICDC descriptions entirely.

This tier is DECA's own format at DECA's own scale. Difficulty comes from the JUDGMENT the
decision demands, never from adding people, figures, or pages. Every rule below is measured
off the 396 real published DECA roleplays in this project's corpus, and the corpus is the
authority wherever your instinct disagrees with it.

Do not announce, label, number, or otherwise refer to any of these rules in your output —
they shape the scenario, they are never mentioned in it.

WHERE ICDC DIFFICULTY ACTUALLY COMES FROM
An ICDC case is not a District case with more moving parts bolted on. It is the same shape,
turned on a harder question. Raise difficulty by these means and no others:
  - the trade-off is between two GOODS, not between a good option and a bad one;
  - the right answer depends on a judgment call the facts genuinely underdetermine;
  - the obvious first move has a real cost that only becomes visible on reflection;
  - the participant must weigh an obligation to one party against an obligation to another.
Raising difficulty by adding a fourth stakeholder, a budget table, or three hundred more
words is EXACTLY the failure this tier exists to prevent.

SECTION ORDER — THIS IS DECA'S ORDER
Output the sections in exactly this order, and put nothing else between them:

  1. the event code / level line, then the event name in capitals
  2. CAREER CLUSTER          (only if this event has one)
  3. INSTRUCTIONAL AREA
  4. 21st CENTURY SKILLS     (only if this event includes it)
  5. PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
  6. the situation section (use the header given in the user message)
  7. JUDGE ROLE-PLAY CHARACTERIZATION, then the numbered judge questions

Do NOT write a PARTICIPANT INSTRUCTIONS section. DECA prints the same block every time and
this pipeline renders it from the event's own configuration; anything you write there is
discarded.

There is no other section. In particular there is no exhibit, no data block, no appendix,
and no attachment — see F3.

F1 — ONE SCENARIO, ONE JUDGE. The case presents a SINGLE business situation, presented to
the one judge who is in the room with the participant. That judge holds a role in the
scenario (owner, supervisor, vice president, client) and is the person being presented to.
Do not write a case that only makes sense if several parties are present. Do not open a
second storyline. The participant has to walk into a room and talk to one person about one
decision; write the case that supports that.

F2 — ROLES, NOT A CAST. Name the COMPANY, in capitals, and any rival or partner company.
Do NOT give personal names to the people in the scenario. Real DECA cases refer to people by
their role — "the owner (judge)", "the applicant", "an employee", "the store manager", "a
supplier" — and roughly three quarters of the corpus names no individual at all. Follow that.

At most TWO parties beyond the judge and the participant have any stake in the decision, and
you do not have to use two. You are not building a set of competing constituencies whose
demands must be reconciled; that is not what this event is. Where a second party's interest
matters, state it in a clause, not in a paragraph of its own.

Every time you refer to the judge's role in the situation and in the judge characterization,
append "(judge)" the way the corpus does: "the owner (judge) has asked you to...".

F3 — NO EXHIBIT, NO DATA BLOCK. Zero of the 396 real roleplays in the corpus carry one.
Never emit a heading beginning "EXHIBIT", never emit a labelled block of figures, and never
attach a table. Any figure the decision genuinely turns on goes in the PROSE, as a plain
sentence.

Be sparing with figures. A real case carries perhaps one or two — "over 200 employees",
"close to 15 years" — and they set the scene rather than drive an arithmetic answer. If the
participant would need a calculator to reach your recommendation, the case is wrong for this
event: rewrite the decision so it turns on judgment instead. Do not construct a budget that
covers all but a remainder, a per-day capacity limit, and a deadline that collides with
another deadline. That is a different exercise wearing this event's headings.

F4 — DECIDABLE. Every fact needed to reason to a defensible recommendation must be ON THE
PAGE. The participant must never need an outside lookup, an industry benchmark you did not
supply, or a number you did not state. Do not ask for a calculation whose inputs are missing.

F5 — NO DOMINANT OPTION. There must be at least 2 genuinely defensible courses of action,
and the prose must make the real cost of each one visible. If a competent competitor could
identify one obviously correct answer in ten seconds, the roleplay has failed. This is where
the difficulty of this tier lives — spend your effort here.

F6 — JUDGE QUESTIONS: exactly as many as the user message's JUDGE QUESTIONS line gives,
no more and no fewer. That is the count DECA's current format prints for this event and
it is checked mechanically.

Write them the way DECA writes them — short, plainly phrased, and answerable from the
scenario. They are conversation openers for the judge, not rubric items. These are real
examples of the register to hit:
  1. Are there any issues you feel are illegal, or just unethical?
  2. Can an employer enforce workplace rules when employees are not at work?
  3. If we hire the applicant or don't hire the applicant, what more should we communicate?

Do NOT write questions in evaluator voice. Do not open a question with "Justify",
"Assess how you would measure", "Weigh", "Critique", or "Defend your recommendation
against". Do not name a stakeholder and quote their position back at the participant. Every
one of those is an artefact of a rubric, and none of them appears in the corpus.

F7 — LENGTH. The situation section alone MUST land between SITUATION_WORD_MIN and
SITUATION_WORD_MAX words, not counting the section header. BOTH ENDS ARE REAL. Aim at
SITUATION_WORD_TARGET. That is the mean length of THIS EVENT's own published cases in the
format DECA prints now, so a situation written to it is the length a competitor actually meets
at this event. The band is computed from that same mean, which is why the top of it is already
the long end of what DECA prints — the maximum is the outer edge of the published range, not
the goal. Over the maximum, the case is longer than any real case of this event and the extra
words are costing the competitor time, not teaching them anything.

Write it as SITUATION_PARA_HINT paragraphs of SITUATION_PARA_WORDS each, on the corpus's own
plan: assign the participant's role and the judge's role; establish the company and the
background the decision turns on; lay out the problem; state what the judge wants analysed,
decided, or recommended; close with the meeting framing. If you are over the maximum, cut
scenario, not sentences — remove a complication rather than compressing prose that is
already doing work.

F8 — NO TELEGRAPHING. The situation must never name, rank, or recommend the answer. Do not
write "the obvious choice", "clearly the best", "the only viable option", or any equivalent.
Present the facts and the competing considerations and stop.

ABOUT THE EXAMPLE ROLEPLAY
The example roleplay, if one is provided, is a real published DECA case. It shows FORMAT,
and at this tier the format is the point — match its shape, its register, its length and its
restraint exactly. What your case must exceed is the DIFFICULTY OF THE JUDGMENT it asks for,
and nothing else. Never reuse its company, its people, or its scenario.

SELF-REPORT TAIL (required)
After the final line of the roleplay, and only there, append this block exactly:

=== SELF-REPORT ===
PARTIES: <role> - <their interest> | <role> - <their interest>
CONSTRAINTS: <constraint> | <constraint>
OPTIONS: <course of action> - <what it really costs> | <course of action> - <what it really costs>
=== END SELF-REPORT ===

Rules for the tail:
  - PARTIES lists everyone with a stake in the decision BESIDES the judge and the
    participant(s). List AT MOST TWO. Listing more is F2 failing, and the tail is where it
    is caught. If nobody else has a stake, write "PARTIES:" and nothing after it.
  - CONSTRAINTS lists what genuinely binds the decision — a policy, a contract, an
    obligation, a commitment already made. There is no minimum and there is no requirement
    that any two of them collide. Do not invent a numeric limit to fill this line.
  - OPTIONS lists the courses of action a competent competitor could defend, with what each
    one really costs. There must be at least TWO. This is F5 and it is the line that matters.
    Every option needs BOTH halves and the " - " between them: the action, and the cost of
    taking it. An option with no stated cost is a failure, not a short entry.
  - Every role, constraint, and option you list must appear in the situation prose. The tail
    is machine-checked against the prose; do not list anything you did not write. THE TWO
    HALVES OF AN OPTION ARE CHECKED SEPARATELY — the cost has to be in the prose too, not
    only the action. If the situation never says what a course of action would cost, the
    situation is where to fix that, not the tail: a reader who cannot see the cost of the
    second option is reading a case with one option in it, which is the failure F5 names.
  - The tail is removed before the roleplay is stored and is never seen by a competitor.
