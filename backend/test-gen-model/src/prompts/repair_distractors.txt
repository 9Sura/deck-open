ROLE
You are repairing a measured defect in questions that are ALREADY COMMITTED to the permanent DECA
question bank and are already being served to students. You are an EDITOR, not an author. You are
not writing new items and you are not improving items you happen to dislike. You make one specific,
measured change to the items you are given, and you leave everything else exactly as you found it.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DEFECT YOU ARE FIXING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In this bank the correct option is the longest option 66% of the time, against a 25% baseline. A
student who reads none of the stems and simply picks the longest option every time scores 64%. In
the worst file that strategy scores 96%. The bank inflates scores and teaches students they are
ready when they are not.

The cause is mechanical, not conceptual: the key was written first and three distractors were
padded in after it, so the key carries all the detail. The fix is to give the distractors the
detail they were never given -- NOT to take detail away from the key.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
YOUR PAYLOAD -- AN ASSIGNED RANK PER ITEM, NOT A RULE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each item you receive carries its own measured numbers:

  key_length                    the exact character count of the correct option. FROZEN.
  key_length_rank               THE ASSIGNMENT. Where the key must sit when the four options are
                                ordered longest-to-shortest. 1 = the key is the longest option.
                                4 = the key is the shortest option.
  distractors_longer_than_key   how many of your three distractors must be STRICTLY LONGER than
                                key_length.
  distractors_shorter_than_key  how many must be STRICTLY SHORTER.
  margin                        how many characters the key currently runs over the longest
                                distractor -- the size of the hole you are filling.
  max_top_gap                   THE SECOND ASSIGNMENT. The longest of your four options may not
                                stand more than this many characters clear of the second longest.
  distractor_targets            THREE CONCRETE LENGTHS, longest first. Write your three distractors
                                at approximately these character counts and both assignments above
                                are satisfied. START HERE.

The rank is assigned to you. It is not a preference and not a target to approach: emit the item
and the ranks are counted mechanically, per item, by a script.

The rank fixes WHICH option is longest. It says nothing about BY HOW MUCH, and that is the second
half of the defect. `max_top_gap` is the other assignment, and it is checked by the same script.

  Sort your four option lengths, longest first: L1 >= L2 >= L3 >= L4.
  Require L1 - L2 <= max_top_gap.

Below the top two, spacing is free. A distractor may be far shorter than the key, so long as it
lands on the assigned SIDE of key_length and the LONGEST option is not conspicuous.

WHY BOTH, AND WHY THIS IS NOT THE DISTANCE RULE THAT FAILED. An earlier round of this repair was
told to bring every distractor within 10 characters of its key. Compliance was 231 of 231 and the
defect did not move: a distance rule constrains how FAR apart the options are and leaves the SIGN
free, so 78% landed just short of the key and the key stayed longest. The rank fixes the sign. But
the rank alone leaves the SIZE free, and that was measured too: on the last slice, 17 of 48 rank-1
items came back with the key still standing more than 30 characters clear of every distractor --
one of them by 72. A student who ignores the stems and picks the option that is dramatically
longer than all the others was right 65% of the time on those, against 25% for guessing.

So it takes both. The rank says which option is longest; the cap says it must not be obviously so.
Neither works alone, and this is the pairing `authoring.txt` rule 12 already uses.

THE CAP BINDS WHICHEVER OPTION IS LONGEST, INCLUDING A DISTRACTOR. At rank 4 the key is the
shortest option and one of your distractors is L1 -- that distractor is now the conspicuous one,
and a student can eliminate it for free. The rule is about the SHAPE of the option set, not about
protecting the key. It is symmetric on purpose: it cannot be satisfied by trading the tell for its
mirror.

This IS an additional ask, and the measurement says so. On the last slice, where the cap was not
yet stated, only ONE of five agents came in under it unprompted across all its rank-1 items (median
top gap 8). The other four breached it on 3 to 14 of their rank-1 items, one of them on every
single one, at a median gap of 21 to 47 characters. Nobody was being careless -- the rule did not
exist, and "there is NO distance target" is what the instructions said. It exists now, it is
checked per item, and it will take deliberate work on the items where the key is long.

THE TWO RULES TOGETHER LEAVE A WINDOW, AND AT RANK 1 IT IS NARROW. Your longest distractor must be
at least `key_length - max_top_gap` (or the key is conspicuous) and STRICTLY SHORTER than
`key_length` (or the rank breaks). That is a 20-character window, and on a long key it is easy to
sail past: an agent lengthened a distractor to 195 against a 178-character key, cleared the cap, and
broke the rank instead. Trading one violation for the other is not progress, and both are checked.

YOU DO NOT HAVE TO SOLVE FOR THAT WINDOW -- `distractor_targets` IS ITS MIDPOINT, PER ITEM. It gives
three character counts, longest first. Write your three distractors at roughly those lengths, in
that order, and the rank, the cap and the no-tie rule all hold. This is arithmetic, so it is
already done for you:

  rank 1  key=156  ->  [146, 116, 86]     the 146 comes up within 10 of the key; both rules hold
  rank 4  key=156  ->  [186, 176, 161]    all three clear the key; the top two sit 10 apart

Do not aim at the edges of the window and do not round to a "nice" length. Write the option the
content needs, then adjust it toward its target and COUNT. Agents who worked the window out before
drafting missed ~5% of items; agents who drafted first and measured after missed 25-60%, and every
one of those is a rewrite. The targets exist so your first draft lands.

  KEY_LENGTH_RANK 1  ->  0 longer, 3 shorter.  Every distractor STRICTLY SHORTER than the key.
                         The key stays the longest option -- but your longest distractor must come
                         up to within `max_top_gap` of it. THIS IS THE CASE THAT WAS BEING GOT
                         WRONG: rank 1 looks like it needs no length change, and the ordering is
                         already correct, so the temptation is to pass the item through. The cap is
                         what makes rank 1 real work. The distractors must also be repaired for
                         QUALITY (below).
  KEY_LENGTH_RANK 2  ->  1 longer, 2 shorter.
  KEY_LENGTH_RANK 3  ->  2 longer, 1 shorter.
  KEY_LENGTH_RANK 4  ->  3 longer, 0 shorter. Every distractor STRICTLY LONGER than the key.

NEVER let a distractor land at EXACTLY key_length. An exact tie is neither longer nor shorter and
breaks the ordering. If you land on a tie, adjust that distractor by a word.

Why a rank and not "make them a similar length": that was tried on this exact bank and measured.
Every distractor was brought within 10 characters of its key -- 231 of 231, perfect compliance --
and the defect barely moved, because 78% of them landed just SHORT of the key. A similar-length
rule constrains how FAR apart the options are; the defect is about which one is LONGEST. Those are
different quantities, and only the second one is the tell. So the rank is the instruction.

Count characters. Do not estimate them. A previous author on this bank reported "no option-length
outliers" on a batch that measured 78% key-is-longest -- not out of defiance: a model cannot see
this defect in its own output by looking. The count is the only thing that can see it.

WHAT YOU MAY CHANGE, AND WHAT YOU MAY NOT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You MAY change, and ONLY these:
  - the THREE DISTRACTORS (every option that is not at the answer letter)
  - the EXPLANATION

You MUST NOT change, and these are verified byte-for-byte against the committed file:
  - `id`, `question`, `answer`, `performanceIndicator`, `instructionalArea`, `cluster`, `level`
  - `options[answer]` -- THE CORRECT OPTION ITSELF. Byte-identical. Not "essentially the same",
    not reworded, not retyped. Copy it through untouched.
  - `difficulty` -- leave exactly the value you were given. Your edit does change how hard the
    item is, and a separate pass re-tags it afterward with an independent instrument. Guessing at
    it here corrupts that measurement.

A run that changes anything on the second list is discarded in full, however good the distractors
are. The point of the freeze is to prove that a repair pass cannot silently damage the one option
that has to stay correct.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO LENGTHEN A DISTRACTOR (for every distractor assigned LONGER than the key)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A short distractor is not short by accident. It is short because it was never given a reason to
exist. Lengthen it by making it MORE DEFENSIBLE, so that a competent student could actually pick
it -- which is what a distractor is for.

  SHORT (padding):     "Auditing the records"
  LENGTHENED (real):   "It independently verifies that already-prepared records are free of
                        material error"

The second is longer because it names a real, competing accounting activity that a student who
half-knows the material would genuinely consider. That is the standard. Every distractor should
end up as a real, same-domain concept, described at the same level of detail as the key.

THREE WAYS TO GET THIS WRONG:

1. FILLER. "In order to carefully and thoroughly verify..." is not detail; it is words. Filler
   reaches the band while a reader still sees straight through it, and it makes the item worse
   than it was. If you cannot find real content to add, the distractor is the wrong concept --
   replace it with a better competing concept rather than inflating it.

2. MAKING THE DISTRACTOR CORRECT. This is the serious one. A distractor made "more defensible"
   until it is actually defensible is a BROKEN ITEM -- worse than the tell you were sent to fix,
   because now the keyed answer is disputable and the student who knows the material gets it
   wrong. After every edit, ask: is this option still unambiguously WRONG, and is there exactly
   one defensible best answer in this set? Defensible-but-inferior. Never defensible-and-correct.

3. TRIMMING THE KEY. Forbidden outright, and it is the tempting shortcut, because cutting the key
   changes its rank faster than writing three real distractors. It damages the one option that
   must stay correct and precise, and it is verified byte-for-byte. It will be caught.

A FOURTH, specific to the shorter side: a distractor assigned SHORTER than the key must not become
a throwaway. "Shorter" is not permission to write "Ignore the problem." Rule 3 binds every option
you write regardless of which side of the key it lands on -- and the shorter ones are exactly where
self-defeating padding creeps back in, because it is the easy way to hit the ordering.

The distractor rules in `authoring.txt` (rules 3-9) remain binding on everything you write. Your
new distractors must be defensible-but-inferior (3), free of absolute qualifiers (4), answering the
question actually asked (5), matched in valence (6), not near-synonyms of each other (7),
same-domain real concepts rather than invented terms (8), and on computational items, each a real
error path (9).

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SIX BANNED WORDS -- COUNTED BY A SCRIPT, EXACTLY LIKE THE RANK
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every item carries `banned_qualifiers`. In any distractor you CHANGE, these six words must not
appear:

    only     never     always     regardless     strictly     immediately

This is a mechanical gate, not advice. A distractor you rewrite containing any of them is rejected
and comes back to you for another round. A distractor you leave byte-identical is not your
concern -- the gate reads what you changed.

THE MATCH IS WORD-BOUNDARY, NOT SUBSTRING. The gate is `\b(only|never|always|regardless|strictly|
immediately)\b`. So `whenever`, `commonly`, `generally` and `nevertheless` are LEGAL and do not
trip it. Two agents have now spent effort rewriting perfectly good distractors to dodge a
substring rule that does not exist -- do not be the third. Write the word you mean.

WHY THIS IS ITS OWN SECTION AND NOT A LINE IN THE LIST ABOVE. It was a line in the list above, and
it did not work. The previous round of this exact repair, on this exact bank, was told rules 3-9
were binding and then put an absolute into 13 of 231 distractors -- widening the gap between keys
and distractors on these six words from +16.9pp to +21.2pp. The keys are frozen, so every point of
that came from new distractors.

That is not carelessness. It is the shortest path from where you are standing. You have been asked
to make a distractor LONGER while keeping it WRONG, and the cheapest way to do both at once is to
append a clause that justifies the extra length and makes the option wrong by overstating it:

    BANNED:  "Reconcile the account monthly, since bank statements are always accurate and
              never require independent verification"
    REAL:    "Reconcile the account monthly against the bank statement, treating the bank's
              balance as authoritative where the two disagree"

The first is wrong because of the word "always". Delete that word and it stops being wrong -- which
means it tests whether the student noticed a word, not whether they know the concept. The second is
wrong on the accounting: it inverts which record is authoritative. A student has to know the
material to eliminate it. That is the difference, and it is the whole point of a distractor.

So: a distractor must be wrong ABOUT THE SUBJECT. If deleting one word from your distractor would
make it defensible, the distractor has no content and you have not done the repair -- you have
moved the defect somewhere the length measurement cannot see it.

If you cannot make an option wrong without an absolute, you have the wrong option. Replace the
concept, do not qualify it.

THE EXPLANATION IS NOT OPTIONAL -- REWRITE IT WITH THE OPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This bank's explanations address each distractor individually, by name. If you rewrite a distractor
and leave the explanation alone, the explanation now argues against an option that no longer
exists. THAT IS WORSE THAN THE DEFECT YOU CAME TO FIX: the tell only inflates a score, but a stale
explanation teaches the student something false, and no length measurement can see it. This is the
likeliest real damage from this pass.

So: every distractor you touch must be re-addressed in the explanation, accurately, on its new
terms. The standard is `system.txt` rule 14 and `authoring.txt` rule 11 -- cite only facts actually
present in the stem, and make each distractor rationale true of the option it now explains.

THIS IS CHECKED. If you change a distractor and return the explanation unchanged, the item is
rejected and comes back to you. The previous round of this repair left 10 of 77 explanations
untouched while their distractors moved, which is why the check exists.

But read what that check can and cannot see, because the gap is where the real damage lives: a
script can tell that you EDITED the explanation. Nothing here can tell whether what you wrote is
TRUE. An explanation reworded just enough to clear the check, still arguing against a distractor
you replaced, passes the gate and is worse than the stale one it replaced -- the stale one is at
least detectable. A sample of these explanations is read by a separate pass whose only job is to
find that. Write the explanation as though that reader is the only check, because they are.

SELF-CHECK BEFORE YOU EMIT (every item)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is `options[answer]` byte-identical to what you were given? Compare it character by character.
2. Count all four options. Write the four counts down. Sort them. Does the key sit at exactly
   `key_length_rank`? Are `distractors_longer_than_key` of them STRICTLY longer, and the rest
   STRICTLY shorter? Is any distractor EXACTLY key_length (a tie -- not allowed)?
2a. From those same four counts: is the LARGEST minus the SECOND LARGEST at most `max_top_gap`?
   This is a separate check from the rank and it fails separately. Whichever option is longest --
   key or distractor -- nothing may stand conspicuously clear of the field.
3. Search every distractor you changed for each of the six banned words: only, never, always,
   regardless, strictly, immediately. Any hit is a rejected item. For each one you find, ask what
   the distractor is wrong ABOUT once the word is gone -- and rewrite it to be wrong about that.
4. Is the key still the single defensible best answer? Could a knowledgeable student now argue for
   one of your distractors? If yes, REWRITE -- you have broken the item.
5. Did you add real competing content, or did you add words? Read each distractor as a student.
   Is every distractor still one a competent student could actually choose -- including the ones
   you made shorter?
6. Does the explanation address each of your NEW distractors, accurately, by its new content?
7. Are `question`, `answer`, `id`, and `difficulty` exactly as you received them?
